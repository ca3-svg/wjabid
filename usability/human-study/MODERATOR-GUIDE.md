# Moderator guide

## Before the session

- Use a clean browser profile and a redacted/synthetic December PDF. Verify the PDF opens and the app starts before the participant arrives.
- Prepare the participant's device, charger, stable connection, and an observation sheet. For remote sessions, test screen sharing without capturing unrelated personal content.
- If recording, confirm the participant separately agreed to recording and knows the deletion date. Stop recording if personal bid information appears unexpectedly.
- Have one moderator and, if possible, one quiet note-taker. Do not let observers coach the participant.

## Opening script

“Thanks for helping. We’re testing the app, not you. There are no wrong actions; if something is unclear, that is useful for us to learn. Please say what you’re looking for and what you expect as you work. I’ll try not to help unless you’re completely stuck. You can stop at any time. We’ll use a sample or redacted bid. May I take notes? Separately: are you comfortable with this session being recorded? You can say no to recording and still take part.”

Confirm the participant has reviewed the information and consent checklist. Do not collect their name or employee number in study notes. Ask only what is useful to interpret the session: usual device/browser, how often they bid, and whether they have used this app before.

## Tasks

Give one card at a time, in order unless a task does not fit the participant's device. Do not read evaluator expectations aloud.

1. Ask them to find two December pairing choices that fit the preference on the card and explain their comparison.
2. Ask them to inspect one pairing and explain what they think each of its two available views is for.
3. Ask them to find a pairing date that works for a sample bid, add it, and handle any conflict the app reports.
4. Ask them to adjust the results toward a stated preference and explain whether that changed the list or only its order. If time allows, save and restore the setup.
5. On their phone/tablet, ask them to load the sample PDF, inspect one pairing, then leave and return to the app.

Skip a task only when its device requirement makes it impossible. Mark it not run; do not substitute a different success criterion.

## Neutral prompts

- “What are you looking for now?”
- “What did you expect to happen?”
- “What does that label mean to you?”
- “What makes you say that?”
- “How sure are you that you chose the right date?”
- If fully stuck: “What would you try next?” After one recovery attempt, offer to move on and record the stopping point.

Avoid “Did you see the filter?”, “Would you click Details?”, or any prompt that names the control needed to finish.

## After each task

Ask the participant to rate:

- Ease: 1 very difficult, 2 difficult, 3 neither, 4 easy, 5 very easy.
- Confidence in the result: 1 not confident, 2 slightly, 3 moderately, 4 confident, 5 very confident.

Then ask: “What, if anything, made that harder than you expected?” Record their words as a quote or close paraphrase, separate from your interpretation.

## Wrap-up

Ask what they would change first, what they would not trust yet, and whether any important bidding step was missing. Thank them; explain how notes will be used and when any recording will be deleted.

## Evaluator-only things to watch

- Whether the participant sees every operating date as a separate choice and keeps the date in view.
- Whether the pairing-number source-text view is distinct from structured Details.
- Whether credit, legs/workday, every rest break, region, and unknown time-zone data are understandable without coaching.
- Whether the participant understands preference targets versus hard filters and can explain a ranking change.
- Whether the exact selected date appears in the bid and conflicts are actionable.
- On mobile: missed taps, accidental horizontal page movement, clipped content, nested scrolling, keyboard covering controls, file picker friction, and state after returning.

Keep these evaluator observations hidden until task completion so the prompt does not teach the participant.
