# Participant task cards

Show one card at a time. These cards describe goals and preferences without naming app controls. The moderator should read them as written and not show the evaluator-only checklist before the task.

## Card 1 — Compare options

You are looking through a December bid. Find two pairing choices that might give you good credit while keeping the flying days manageable and the breaks between flights generous. Show me how you would compare them and tell me which one you would look at more closely.

## Card 2 — Understand a pairing

Choose one date for a pairing you are considering. Look through the information available for it. Tell me what you think each view is showing and what you would use it for.

## Card 3 — Choose a date for a sample bid

For this exercise, December 15 works for you and December 22 does not. Add a suitable pairing choice for December 15 to the sample bid. Check the result and deal with any issue the app reports. Do not submit anything outside this sample app.

## Card 4 — Change what matters to you

For this exercise, you prefer about one flight on each day you work and longer breaks between flights. Adjust the results to reflect that. Tell me whether you think the change removes options or changes their order. If you can, save the setup and find it again.

## Card 5 — Use the app on your device

Using the sample bid file on this device, find a pairing, inspect it, leave the app, and come back. Tell me what was easy, difficult, or missing.

## Moderator setup notes

- The exact operating date in Card 3 is intentional: the app must preserve separate recurrence dates. Make sure the loaded sample PDF includes that date; otherwise use a matching date and annotate the change before the session.
- Card 1 intentionally leaves “good credit” and “manageable” to the participant's interpretation. Ask what those mean to them; do not supply numbers.
- Card 4 tests whether the participant understands a ranking preference. Do not name a slider, filter, profile, or control.
- If a task cannot be completed because the sample data lacks an appropriate pairing, mark it as a data limitation, not a participant failure.
